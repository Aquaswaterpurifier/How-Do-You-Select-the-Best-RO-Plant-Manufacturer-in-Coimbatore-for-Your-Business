## **4. Ask for Raw Water Testing Before Plant Design**

One common mistake businesses make is purchasing an RO plant without understanding their raw water quality.

In Coimbatore, water characteristics can vary depending on the location and source. Borewell water, municipal water and other sources may have different TDS, hardness, iron content and dissolved contaminants.

Before finalising your RO plant, conduct a proper raw water analysis.

Important parameters include:

- Total Dissolved Solids (TDS).
- pH level.
- Total hardness.
- Turbidity.
- Iron content.
- Chloride levels.
- Relevant heavy metals.
- Microbiological quality, where applicable.

Why is this important?

Because the raw water analysis determines the treatment process required for your business.

For example, water with high hardness may require suitable pretreatment. Water containing iron may need additional treatment before entering the RO membrane system.

A professional RO plant manufacturer should review your water test report before recommending equipment.

**Never select an industrial RO plant based only on the TDS reading.** Complete water analysis is important for proper system design.

## **5. Evaluate the Quality of RO Membranes and Equipment**

The performance of an RO plant depends on the quality of its components and how well the complete system is designed.

Before purchasing, ask the manufacturer about the specifications of the important equipment.

**RO Membranes**

RO membranes are responsible for separating much of the dissolved material from water. Ask about membrane specifications, operating conditions, compatibility and replacement availability.

**High-Pressure Pumps**

The high-pressure pump supplies the pressure required for the RO process. Check the pump specifications and suitability for your raw water conditions.

**Sand Filters**

These filters help remove suspended particles and sediments before water enters the RO system.

**Activated Carbon Filters**

They help reduce chlorine, odour and certain organic impurities, depending on the design and media condition.

**Micron Cartridge Filters**

These filters provide additional protection against fine suspended particles.

**Control Panel**

A suitable control panel helps operators monitor and manage the system's operation.

A trustworthy manufacturer should be able to explain the specifications, quality and function of the major components.

## **6. Compare the Complete Treatment Process, Not Just the RO Machine**

An industrial RO plant is not just a membrane and pump assembly. It requires a properly designed treatment process.

Depending on your raw water quality and application, the system may include:

1. Raw water storage tank.
2. Feed water pump.
3. Pressure sand filter.
4. Activated carbon filter.
5. Water softener or antiscalant dosing system, where required.
6. Micron cartridge filter.
7. High-pressure pump.
8. RO membrane assembly.
9. Treated water storage tank.
10. Post-treatment or disinfection equipment, where required.

Not every business needs all these components. The design should be based on water analysis and the final water quality requirements.

When comparing manufacturers, ask for a complete process flow diagram.

This helps you understand what equipment is included and whether any important treatment stage has been left out.

## **7. Check Energy Efficiency and Water Recovery**

An industrial RO plant operates using pumps, filters, membranes and other electrical equipment. Therefore, electricity consumption and water recovery are important factors when comparing manufacturers.

**Electricity Consumption**

Ask the manufacturer about:

- Total connected electrical load.
- Expected power consumption.
- Pump efficiency.
- Operating pressure.
- Automation and control features.

A properly designed system can help reduce unnecessary energy consumption.

**Water Recovery**

Water recovery refers to the percentage of feed water converted into treated water (permeate).

For example, if a system receives 1,000 litres of feed water and produces 650 litres of treated water, its recovery is 65%.

The remaining water is reject or concentrate water.

The achievable recovery depends on raw water chemistry, membrane design, scaling potential and operating conditions.

Ask your manufacturer to explain the expected recovery percentage and how reject water will be managed.

Do not choose a system based only on a high recovery claim. It must be technically suitable for your raw water.

## **9. Evaluate Installation and After-Sales Service**

Purchasing an RO plant is only the beginning. Proper installation, commissioning and maintenance are essential for long-term performance.

Imagine your factory depends on purified water for daily production. If the RO system suddenly stops working, it may interrupt your operations.

This is why after-sales service is an important factor when selecting a manufacturer in Coimbatore.

Before purchasing, check whether the company provides:

**Professional Installation:** Proper equipment installation, piping connections, electrical integration and commissioning.

**Operator Training:** Guidance on operating the plant, monitoring performance and handling basic maintenance.

**Regular Maintenance:** Scheduled inspection of filters, membranes, pumps and other components.

**Spare Parts Availability:** Easy access to replacement membranes, cartridge filters, pumps and other essential components.

**Technical Support:** Assistance when the plant experiences pressure variations, low output or water quality issues.

A manufacturer who provides clear service commitments can help you manage your plant more confidently.

Two manufacturers may quote different prices for what appears to be the same capacity RO plant. However, their membrane specifications, pumps, pretreatment systems, automation and installation scope may be different.

When comparing quotations, check the following:

## **14. Quality Standards and Compliance for RO Plant Buyers**

Water quality should always be a priority when purchasing an RO plant.

However, the applicable standards depend on the intended use of the treated water.

For general drinking water applications, the BIS drinking water specification IS 10500 is an important reference. Industrial processes may require different specifications depending on the application.

If you are planning to manufacture and sell packaged drinking water, additional food safety requirements apply.

For example:

- Appropriate FSSAI registration or licence.
- Compliance with applicable packaged drinking water standards.
- Prescribed source water and finished product testing.
- Hygienic manufacturing and packaging practices.
- Proper quality control documentation.
